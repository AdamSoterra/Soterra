# More free content Soterra could ingest

## The short version

- **Australia's National Construction Code is CC BY 4.0 and the ABCB has said in writing that the NCC may be used to train AI.** That is ~2,241 pages of NCC 2025 plus 2,057 pages of ABCB handbooks, available as clause-structured XML with stable IDs. Nothing else on this list comes close.
- **The NSW Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2017 (70pp, CC BY 4.0) is the single best-shaped document found.** Every entry is "X is defective if..." plus a number plus a measurement method. It converts to pre-inspection checklist items almost mechanically. Queensland has its own version (60pp, also CC BY) so you can cross-check tolerance numbers before quoting them.
- **On the NZ side the pickings are thinner than expected.** LBP Codewords (128 articles) is the cleanest grant anywhere, and the Taumata Arowai drinking-water Acceptable Solutions are secondary legislation so they carry no copyright at all. The MBIE Tolerances guide is amber, not green, because its best numbers are restatements of Standards NZ material.
- **Two things you would have assumed were fine are not:** the MBIE earthquake-prone-buildings methodology carries its own non-commercial wording that overrides the site licence, and the NSW Building Defects Library, which is the most perfectly inspection-shaped document in the whole search, is legally untouchable.
- **The biggest single lever is not new research at all.** Roughly 1,100 MBIE determinations plus the whole legislation set were cleared in July and never loaded. That is pure backlog.

**Two operational rules for the whole ABCB set, and they are not optional.**

1. **Read the licence page of every single ABCB PDF before ingesting it.** The handbook category currently serves `Handbook-landslide-hazards-2015.pdf`, which is all-rights-reserved, sitting in the same list as the 20 CC BY handbooks with no visual distinction. The standards category likewise mixes CC BY-ND with all-rights-reserved. The ABCB's own copyright page says the licence "will vary according to the type of material". Pre-2019 vintage is the tell.
2. **Do not point a crawler at abcb.gov.au or ncc.abcb.gov.au.** Both serve a robots.txt section headed "Block common AI/training crawlers" that disallows ClaudeBot, GPTBot, anthropic-ai and others. This does not affect the CC BY licence on content you lawfully obtain, but acquisition must be targeted one-off downloads of known files. The ABCB blessed the use while blocking the crawl.

**Three more scoping rules:**
- **Never scrape the NCC 2022 HTML preface pages.** They still display a CC BY-ND claim today, contradicting the PDFs, the data.gov.au README and the ABCB FAQ. PDFs or XML only.
- **Strip Standards Australia extracts.** The NSW guide (SAI Global licence 1407-c122, pages 18, 24, 26, 32, 49), the QBCC guide (licence CLT1123qbc, pages 12, 17, 18, 19, 26, 31, 42) and the ABCB handbooks (e.g. Condensation p17 reproduces AS 4200.1:2017 with "Users must not copy or reuse this work") all embed AS content under licences that run to the publisher only. The ACT notes are the only clean set.
- **Do not serve NCC figures or any ABCB/NCC logo.** Images, photographs and trade marks are carved out of the CC BY grant in the NCC PDFs and XML, and the AI FAQ forbids implying endorsement. Text only.

## Seismic: a seam that just closed

Worth flagging as a strategy change rather than a single verdict. The Seismic Assessment of Existing Buildings guidelines (12 PDFs plus 2 templates) are jointly managed by **five** parties, not four: MBIE, EQC, NZSEE, SESOC and the NZ Geotechnical Society. SESOC is already on your caution list. The usual fallback was "take the clean MBIE-only half instead", meaning the EPB methodology, and that has now gone red on MBIE's own non-commercial wording. So there is no clean seismic path left short of a five-signature permission. Deprioritise seismic entirely rather than chasing it.

## If I were picking

1. **Load the July backlog first, especially the ~1,100 MBIE determinations.** Highest value per hour on the whole page, zero legal work outstanding, NZ-specific, and it directly strengthens the answers you are already giving. Nothing new should start before this ships.
2. **NSW Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2017.** 70 pages, CC BY 4.0, no crawler restrictions, no acquisition constraints, and it is already in checklist shape. Highest value-to-effort ratio of any new find. Strip the five SAI Global pages, then cross-check the numbers against the QBCC guide before any of them reach a paying user.
3. **The NCC as XML, from data.gov.au.** The big one, and the XML route means clause IDs rather than page numbers plus no PDF extraction error. Take the ABCB handbooks in the same pass. Budget for it honestly: this is a large ingest, it needs per-file licence checking, per-document Standards Australia extract stripping, figure exclusion, and separate versioning for NCC 2022 vs 2025 editions of the same handbook. It is not a weekend.
4. **Codewords, plus the ACT Construction Notes.** Two small, clean, fast wins that punch above their size. Codewords is the cleanest grant anywhere and fills your regulator-obligations gap; the ACT notes are the only tolerance-adjacent set with zero Standards Australia contamination, and they are written in the "failure observed, clause cited, fix stated" shape your product outputs.
5. **Send two emails and open one web page.** The page: someone opens plan.sa.gov.au in a normal browser and reads the footer, which unlocks South Australia in ten seconds. The emails: Kāinga Ora (best NZ target, blanket "ask first" with no anti-commercial position) and BCA Singapore (one email covering both CONQUAS and the Good Industry Practices guides, and CONQUAS is the most inspection-native document found anywhere).

**What to do first, concretely:** ship the backlog, then NSW, then start the NCC job. Send the three permission asks in parallel on day one, since they cost nothing to have in flight while the ingest work proceeds.

**Before any of it goes live, two rules need to exist in the pipeline, not just in a document.** First, per-file licence checking, because the ABCB, HSE and MBIE all mix licences within a single site and in two cases within a single category listing. Second, a hard retrieval rule that the assistant never attempts to supply the content of a referenced standard, AS/NZS or BS or ASTM alike, and says so plainly instead. That second one is where well-meaning gap-filling would create real statutory exposure, and it is the same discipline that saved you on the Standards NZ handbook.
