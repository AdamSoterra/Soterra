## 2. What Soterra is

**One sentence:** Soterra is an AI assistant for New Zealand construction that answers a site question from the project's own drawings and the New Zealand Building Code, with a citation to the exact source page.

**The longer version.** A construction project runs on two bodies of documents nobody can search at speed: the project's own drawings and specifications, and the Building Code plus the manufacturer manuals it refers to. When someone on site needs an answer, the options today are to guess, to ring the architect and wait, or to book another inspection. Soterra reads all of it and answers in seconds, and every answer shows the page it came from so the answer can be checked rather than trusted.

**What it does today, all live:**

- **Cited question answering** from the project's uploaded drawings, the full NZ Building Code, 364 MBIE determinations, and manufacturer technical literature held under permission.
- **QA checklists.** Ask for a pre-line check and it generates a real tickable list, each item cited to the drawing, clause or manual behind it. Crew tick it off on a phone on site, with a photo and a note per item.
- **Health and safety plans.** Generates a Safe Work Method Statement for a task, grounded in HSWA 2015 and WorkSafe practice.
- **RFI drafting.**
- **A CCC evidence pack**, a 36 item Code Compliance Certificate checklist covering producer statements, energy work certificates, LBP records of work and as-builts.
- **Insights**, which reads a company's own past inspection reports and surfaces what they keep failing on. This is the least mature part of the product. Do not lead with it.

**Platforms:** web at soterra.co.nz, an Android app, and on iPhone it installs from Safari as a home screen app. There is deliberately no iOS App Store release.

## 4. The evidence base

These are Adam's own findings and they are the strongest material available. Use them.

- On **one Auckland consent** there were **27 inspections**. Only **22% passed clean**. **67% needed a return visit.** Every failed item was either an "as per plan" drawing lookup or a Building Code figure. The drawings were on site the whole time and it changed nothing.
- **$995 million a year** is the cost of defects in New Zealand construction (NZIER). Do not use the $2.5 billion figure, that is the size of the whole market opportunity, not the defect cost.
- **B1 structural is 32% of Auckland RFIs.**
- MBIE's own survey: **96% of building consent authorities** say applicants expect the council to do the quality checking for them.

**Accuracy rules on these numbers:**

- Say "27 inspections across a single Auckland consent". Do not say "27 council reports", it implies 27 projects.
- The 22% and 67% come from that one consent. Do not attach them to anything else.

## 5. Where the business actually is

Be honest about this in anything you write. Overclaiming is the fastest way to lose credibility in a small market where everyone knows everyone.

- **Live and working**, used on real jobs, but **no paying customer yet** as at 10 August 2026. Pricing is set but not yet transacted.
- **Pricing:** per user per month. Companies $79, dropping to $69 and $59 at volume. Councils $99 and $89. A $500 onboarding fee covers loading their documents. Unlimited questions, no credits, no per question charge.
- **Standards New Zealand licensing conversation is live and warm.** Contact is David Riley, Senior Advisor Copyright and Licensing. Soterra wants a licence to cite NZS standards (NZS 3604 above all, the timber framing standard).
  - ⚠️ **Never describe New Zealand building standards as "paywalled".** They are not. MBIE sponsors free view and print access. What is restricted is *reproduction*. Getting this wrong would be embarrassing in front of anyone in the industry.
- **Manufacturer permissions** are the other pillar. GIB, BOSS Fire and Kingspan Thermakraft literature is in the product pending written agreement. Others are at various stages. The moat is this New Zealand layer: the Code, council inspection practice, and the manufacturers a job actually specifies. Global tools have no reason to build it.
- **Deliberately not building Procore integrations.** That was considered and rejected.
- Target customers are New Zealand commercial construction companies and, separately, councils and building consent authorities.

## 7. Things to never claim

- That Soterra has paying customers or revenue, unless Adam tells you that has changed.
- That Soterra is SOC 2 or ISO certified. **Its providers are** (Vercel, Clerk, Neon, Anthropic, all SOC 2 Type II). Soterra itself is not, and the distinction matters to a procurement person.
- That standards are paywalled. See above.
- That the product integrates with Procore or anything else. It does not.
- Any specific manufacturer's endorsement. Permission to cite literature is not endorsement.
- That data is stored in New Zealand. The database is in **Sydney**.
