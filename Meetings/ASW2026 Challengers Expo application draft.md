## 3. The single strongest "rebels and renegades" angle

**The angle: construction decided that knowing things is a person, not a document. Soterra says the answer was already written down, and gets it to the wall in ten seconds.**

Say it as the line in Q3: *"The industry decided the answer lives in someone's head."*

Why this one, over the other two candidates:

- **It is a structural problem, not a feature gap.** The brief asks for founders who looked at their industry and said the system is broken. "Documents are hard to search" is a feature gap. "An entire trade runs on recall, and when recall fails the fix is to book another inspection" is a broken system, with a cost attached: 67% return visits on that consent, and NZIER's $995m a year in defects nationally.
- **It has first-party evidence.** You are not asserting the rebellion, you are producing 27 council reports where the drawings were physically on site and it changed nothing. That single detail, the documents were there and nobody checked, is the most quotable thing in your whole application. It converts an opinion into a finding.
- **It names an establishment that is comfortable.** Rework is billable. Reinspection fees are council revenue. Knowledge in senior heads is job security. Nobody in the chain was hurting enough to fix it. That is exactly the "look the establishment in the eye" framing they used.
- **A non-construction audience gets it in one sentence.** The curators are startup people, not builders. "The answer was in the file the whole time" needs no domain knowledge.

**The non-coder solo founder** is your strongest personal story and your weakest application story. There is no team or founder question on this form, so it has nowhere to live in the written application without sounding like an excuse. Hold it for the stand and for media, where it is genuinely striking: a project manager who is not a developer built a working retrieval system over the Building Code because the developers who could have were not going to bother with New Zealand. Frame it as velocity and domain proximity, never as apology.

**Refusing the integration playbook** is your investor answer, not your expo answer. "We deliberately did not build Procore integrations, because the moat is the New Zealand code and manufacturer layer that no global tool has a reason to build" is sharp with anyone who knows the category, and meaningless to everyone else. Keep it in the back pocket for the investor conversations at the stand.

## 4. What to physically show at the stand

Four hours, mixed crowd, most of whom have never read a construction drawing. The stated criterion is *"Interactive: ability to showcase your product/service in a visually appealing demo setup"*, so this is scored, and it is where you actually beat the other 24 stands.

**The centrepiece: a physical stack of drawings next to a phone.**

Print or borrow a real plan set, A1 or A3, a few hundred pages, and put it on the table as a physical object. Then the phone. The whole pitch is a gesture: the answer to this question is somewhere in that stack. Find it. You cannot. Watch. Non-construction people understand a 500 page paper stack instantly, and no screenshot achieves this.

**The 30 second live demo, done out loud, on repeat.**

1. Visitor picks a question card off the table.
2. You hold the phone up and speak the question. Voice matters here, it reads as "at the wall" rather than "at a desk".
3. Answer appears on the phone and mirrors to a large monitor behind you.
4. The citation card opens and the actual source page image appears, highlighted.

Step 4 is the moment. The answer is not the wow, the receipt is. A stranger who knows nothing about construction still understands "it showed me the page it got that from", and that is also the exact thing that separates you from ChatGPT in their head. Do not let anyone leave without seeing a source page render.

**Let them drive.** Hand the phone over. Have six pre-written question cards so a visitor with no construction knowledge has something to say, mixing one obviously technical ("what size lintel do I need over a 2.4m opening") with one anyone can grasp ("how far apart do the screws go on this wall"). A stand where the public touches the product outscores a stand where the founder demos at them.

**On the back wall, three things only.**

1. One anonymised council inspection report, blown up, with the failed lines highlighted and the words "as per plan" circled. This is your artefact. It is real, it is local, and it is the argument.
2. Three numbers, large: **27 inspections. 22% passed clean. 67% came back.**
3. One sentence: **"The drawings were on site the whole time."**

**Practical:**

- Anonymise the reports properly. Those PDFs carry real names, emails and phone numbers of council and site staff, flagged in PRODUCT-STUDY.md. Do not put an un-redacted one on a public wall or in a photo a journalist takes.
- Record a screen video of the full demo as a fallback and have it looping, in case GridAKL's wifi is loaded with 25 stands on it. Prefer live, but do not be dead in the water.
- Bring two charged phones, a charger, and a long HDMI. Confirm with GridAKL whether power and a screen are provided, none of the stand logistics are published.
- Have one clean, agreed sentence ready about NZS standards licensing. You are mid-conversation with Standards New Zealand, and a public stand plus media is exactly where someone asks. Never describe standards as paywalled.
- Do not print a price list. Per-seat pricing is in the application, that is enough.

## 5. Honest assessment, and the one fix

**Likelihood: better than the average applicant, but with one specific hole.**

Scored against their five published criteria:

| Criterion | Soterra |
|---|---|
| Challenging industry status quo | Very strong. Evidence-backed, and construction is under-represented against the usual consumer and fintech applicants, which helps a curator building a varied floor. |
| Interactive demo | Very strong. Voice question, cited source page, physical plan stack. Better than most SaaS dashboards. |
| Innovative / tech-enabled | Strong. |
| Auckland-based | Strong, assuming it is true. |
| **Growth stage, "operating and generating revenue"** | **Weak. This is the hole.** |

This is a curation, not a judged competition. No panel, no rubric, no scoring published. Curators building a 25 stand floor optimise for a room that looks impressive to investors and media, which means they want variety, a demo that draws a crowd, and enough traction that the startup does not embarrass them. You win the first two comfortably. The third is where they will hesitate, because "operating and generating revenue" is the criterion they wrote down first and you would be answering it with product maturity instead of customers.

My honest call: **around 35 to 40% as the application stands today. Around 60 to 65% with one paying customer named.**

**The one thing that would most improve this application: get one paying customer, or one signed paid pilot, before 3 September, and state it in a single sentence with a number.**

Something like: *"Three seats at $79 per user with [company], paying since August, on a live Auckland job."* One sentence. It converts you from a strong demo into "in-market and gaining traction", which is the literal phrasing on the event page and the only criterion you currently miss. You have roughly three and a half weeks, the product is live on three platforms, pricing is locked, and you already have warm construction contacts. That is a realistic three week goal and it is worth more than any rewrite of the words above.

If it genuinely cannot happen by 3 September, the fallback is a named company using it on a live job with written permission to name them, plus a signed letter of intent, and say exactly that without dressing it as revenue. Do not inflate this. It is a council-run programme, the people curating it are inside the Auckland ecosystem, and a claim you cannot stand behind in October at the stand is worse than a modest one in September.

1. **Exhibitor cost is not published anywhere.** "Free" on the listing applies to attendees. Email info@aucklandstartupweek.com or ask GridAKL directly whether exhibitors pay, and what the stand includes, before you plan a build.
