# The standards demo, and the council opportunity

**Date: 3 August 2026. Audience: Adam. Status: decision-ready.**

---

## Read this first: the premise is wrong, and fixing it makes the pitch stronger

The brief says NZS 3604 and friends are "paywalled by Standards NZ". They are not.

MBIE sponsors free PDF access to 134 building standards as at the official December 2024 list. NZS 3604:2011, NZS 4229:2013, NZS 3101.1&2:2006, NZS 3602:2003, NZS 4251.1:2007, NZS 4223.3:2016, NZS 4514 (2009 and 2021), NZS 2295:2006, NZS 4219:2009, NZS 1170.5:2004 (excl. A1) and NZS 3404 Parts 1 and 2:1997 are all on it. Each carries the same wording on its shop page:

> "The Building System Performance branch of MBIE has sponsored access to view and print a single downloadable PDF copy of this standard at no charge."

And directly under it:

> "You are not permitted to reproduce or distribute any part of this standard without prior written permission from Standards New Zealand."

That second sentence is the whole business. **The access barrier is already gone, paid for by the Crown. The only thing left to negotiate is the reproduction right.** That is a materially smaller ask than "unlock a paywall", it costs Standards NZ no sales revenue on the sponsored titles, and it is exactly what their published Digital Products Licensing programme exists to grant.

Three practical consequences:

1. **Delete the phrase "which is paywalled" from every Soterra fallback string before recording anything.** Any BCO, BOINZ member or Standards NZ staffer watching the video will catch it in the first thirty seconds and everything after that is discounted.
2. The replacement line is: *"That figure is in NZS 3604:2011, Section 8. The standard is free to download from Standards NZ. Soterra cannot reproduce the table for you yet because we are licensing that content."* Honest, accurate, and it makes the gap look like a deal in progress rather than a product hole.
3. It reframes the ask from "give us access" to "let us index and cite what the Crown already gives away", which is a much easier yes.

## 1. Pick this question

### The recommendation: the lintel question

**Type it exactly like this:**

> **"What size lintel do I need over a 2.4 m garage door opening in a single storey house?"**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What Soterra answers today, Building Code only** | Names B1 Structure as the performance clause. Confirms B1/AS1 routes a compliant timber-framed house to NZS 3604:2011. Explains that the answer is driven by span, loaded dimension (roof and floor load width) and site wind zone. Names NZS 3604:2011 Section 8 (Walls) as the section holding the lintel selection tables, and flags that Section 16 separately holds Composite Construction Lintel Tables so the builder knows which set applies. Links to the free sponsored PDF. |
| **What is missing without the standard** | The member size and grade. Nothing in the Building Code, any Acceptable Solution, or any manufacturer manual in the corpus supplies it. The answer terminates at "check the NZS 3604 lintel tables". |
| **What the answer becomes with the standard** | The exact table row for that span, loaded dimension and wind zone, returned as a cited extract with the rendered page image, table number and edition stamp, plus a link to the free sponsored PDF and the standing line that the standard itself is the authority. *(The actual figure is deliberately not written in this briefing, because we have not read it out of the standard for reproduction.)* |

**Why this one, hard:**

**The users asked for it by name.** BRANZ research into AI in consenting records, verbatim: *"BCOs are also interested in AI tools that can quickly interpret specifications, standards, and compliance data, such as NZS 3604 lintel sizes or material compatibility under E2/AS1."* Building consent officers told a Crown research institute they want this exact product and named this exact lookup. Nothing else in the pack comes close to that as validation. Sourcing note: the article I have this from carries no visible publication date, byline Ben O'Connell. BRANZ's own Build magazine has a companion piece, "Progress towards AI-assisted consenting". **Use the Build article as the citation in anything formal.**

**It sits in the largest RFI category, and it is the named root cause.** B1 Structure is 32% of Building Code clause line items in Auckland RFIs and 31% in Tauranga. E2 is 19% and 25%. Together they are 51% Auckland and 56% Tauranga, so "over half" holds in both councils. The University of Auckland ModelDocs work (BRANZ-funded, Prof Anthony Hōete, May 2023 dwelling consent data from Auckland Council and Tauranga City Council) identifies the number one B1 cause as *"generic referencing to NZS3604 but not referring to specific tables and values"*, with the omissions named as stud height, stud spacing and the correct number of bracing units. **Soterra's current answer to this question is literally the defect.** That is a devastating and honest demo framing: today the assistant reproduces the number one documented cause of consent delay, and a licence turns it into the fix.

**The money is measured, not extrapolated.** In Auckland, applications take a median 55 working days end to end against 19 working days of actual council processing, with roughly 36 days waiting on RFI responses, and about 30 RFI line items per application. Designers average 1.2 days per line item. Use those. Do **not** lead with the 600,000+ national RFI line items figure; the source itself calls it an extrapolation from two councils.

**The gap is unarguable.** There is no free BRANZ page that restates lintel tables compactly, no free map that supplies the missing input, no manufacturer literature substitute. Compare that to head flashing lap, where BRANZ publishes the answer free on its own website, or wind zone, where BRANZ Maps returns an approximate zone for any NZ address with no login.

**It lands with both audiences, differently and correctly:**
- *To Standards NZ:* Standards NZ already publishes **SNZ HB 3604:2011, "Timber-framed buildings - Selected extracts from NZS 3604:2011"**, described as a collection of figures and tables commonly used on-site, carrying the disclaimer that it must not be used as a substitute for the full Standard. That is the product Soterra is asking to build, in paper, already sold by them, with the disclaimer already attached. Lead the licensing conversation with that handbook. It converts the ask from novel to precedented in one sentence.
- *To a council:* it is the number one RFI category, and Auckland Council's own published RFI list names NZS 3604 by section.

### Backup question 1: safety glass

> **"Does this window need safety glass?"**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Today** | F2 Hazardous Building Materials is the performance clause. F2/AS1 clause 1.1.1 is a single sentence and Soterra can quote it in full because it is Crown content: *"Glazing likely to be subject to human impact shall comply with NZS 4223: Part 3."* Soterra can name NZS 4223.3:2016 as the current cited edition and note the superseded 1993 and 1999 editions are also sponsored. |
| **Missing** | Everything. Every threshold: sill height, pane area, proximity to doors, wet-area rules, glass grade. F2/AS1 is a pure cross-reference containing no numbers at all. |
| **With the standard** | The applicable clause and table from NZS 4223.3:2016, cited, with page image, so the call is made before the glass is ordered rather than after it is installed. |

**This has the purest gap in the whole set.** The Acceptable Solution contributes one sentence, so there is zero risk of an expert in the room saying "that is already in the Acceptable Solution", which is the failure mode that kills several other candidate questions. It is also an injury risk rather than a paperwork risk, and it is caught late, when the remedy is replacing glass on a finished house.

Its weakness against the lintel question is frequency evidence: I have no verified RFI or inspection-failure rate for glazing. Do not claim one.

### Backup question 2: bracing units

> **"How many bracing units does this wall line need?"**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Today** | Names B1 and B1/AS1's route to NZS 3604. Names Section 5 (Bracing design) as the demand section and Section 8 (Walls) as where element capacity ratings sit. **Already returns GIB bracing system capacities** from manufacturer literature in the corpus. |
| **Missing** | The demand side. The wind and earthquake bracing demand tables in Section 5. Soterra can say what a GIB panel is worth but not how many the wall line needs, so it cannot close the loop between demand and capacity. |
| **With the standard** | Demand from Section 5 set against capacity from manufacturer literature already held, in a single answer, both cited. |

**This is the only question where the licence completes an answer Soterra is already half way through.** That is the best argument to Standards NZ that a licence multiplies existing value rather than creating a new silo. It also carries the sharpest council hook: Auckland Council's own published common-RFI page says, verbatim, *"Ensure proposed Bracing satisfies all the requirements of section 5 of NZS3604:2011 or provide SED."* The council names the section the licence would unlock.

Two accuracy notes. Frame the demo on **demand**, not on "it cannot give the BU number", or a technical viewer will contradict you with the GIB capacity tables. And bracing is explicitly excluded from the current DZ NZS 3604 revision round, so the 2011 provisions stay live and will not be superseded mid-negotiation.

### Backup question 3: notching

> **"Can I notch this top plate for the plumbing run, and how deep?"**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Today** | Almost nothing. Limits exist in NZS 3604 Section 8; exceeding them needs specific design or a proprietary strap. For a GIB-braced element the manufacturer hole limits apply and are already held. |
| **Missing** | Permitted depth and width, and the reinforcing requirement. No Acceptable Solution covers structural cut-outs. E2/AS1 is an external moisture document and is silent. |
| **With the standard** | The notching and drilling limits for studs and plates, cited, with the reinforcing condition. |

BRANZ SR335 lists "Frame cut-outs" as a genuine compliance defect, verbatim: *"Cut-out is larger than NZS3604 allows and not reinforced. Mainly in top and bottom plates. A structural issue."* It is decided in seconds by a plumber with a hole saw and buried behind linings within days.

**Demo hygiene: pose it about a plain loadbearing stud or plate, not a braced panel**, or Soterra answers it from GIB literature and the demo collapses.

### Backup question 4: smoke alarm placement

> **"Where exactly do the smoke alarms go in this house?"**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Today** | C/AS1 (risk group SH) clause 2.2.1.2 requires a minimum Type 1 domestic smoke alarm system in compliance with NZS 4514, and Appendix C.2.1.1 describes what a Type 1 system is. Soterra can quote both in full. |
| **Missing** | Every placement dimension. Distance from walls and corners, distance from cooking appliances, coverage per level, bedroom and egress rules. All inside NZS 4514. |
| **With the standard** | The placement clauses cited with page image, plus edition handling, since both NZS 4514:2009 and NZS 4514:2021 sit on the current sponsored list and the assistant must know which edition the consent is under. |

**Hard correction before you use it: for a house it is C/AS1, not C/AS2.** C/AS2 covers apartments, hotels, backpackers and community sleeping spaces. Saying C/AS2 in front of a council fire reviewer is an unforced error.

### The control question, and you must include it

> **"How much clearance do I need from the bottom of the cladding to the path?"**

Fully answerable today, no licence involved. E2/AS1 4th edition clause 9.1.2.7: minimum 100 mm above a paved surface, 175 mm above finished unpaved surface, plus the 50 mm slab overlap and 6 mm horizontal offset for direct-fixed claddings, backed by the by-cladding-type rows of Table 9.1.2.1. Cited extract, rendered page image.

Its job in the video is to show Standards NZ and the council what a complete Soterra answer looks like end to end, so every gap that follows reads as a licensing gap and not a product gap. **Open with it.**

Two things: cite **4th edition numbering** (clause 9.1.2.7, Table 9.1.2.1), not the 3rd edition "Table 18" that is still all over council checklists. And ask it about a **concrete slab**. Ask the same question about a suspended timber floor and Table 9.1.2.1 note (1) hands straight off to NZS 3604, which is a bonus beat if you want it.

### Questions that would damage the demo. Do not use any of these.

- **Pool barrier heights.** F9/AS1 contains all the dimensions itself as free Crown content Soterra already holds: 1200 mm barrier height, 1800 mm mesh and boundary barriers, 100 mm sphere, 1500 mm latch release. NZS 8500 is cited at exactly one place, for assessing barrier strength. A council pool inspector knows F9/AS1 by heart. This one slide would end the meeting.
- **H1.2 vs H3.1 framing treatment.** B2/AS1 already answers it and actively modifies the NZS 3602:2003 tables (minimum H1.2 for radiata and Douglas fir framing, H3.1 removed as a structural or framing option). Demonstrating it as unanswerable is visibly wrong.
- **Mortar joint thickness.** MBIE's Guide to Tolerances publishes "average thickness of 10 mm ± 3 mm (NZS 4210:2001)" under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. And that guide disclaims the compliance framing: it covers aesthetic issues rather than Building Code non-compliance.
- **Window head flashing lap.** BRANZ publishes the compact rule free: 35 mm minimum, or 60 mm in an extra high wind zone. Soterra can already give a near-complete answer from E2/AS1 Table 4.5.1.1.
- **"We cannot tell you your wind zone."** BRANZ Maps is free, browser-based, no login, returns an approximate wind zone on an 80 x 80 m grid nationwide plus corrosion zone. The defensible framing is narrower: what is licensed is the Section 5 determination method and the zone definitions, not the mere fact of the zone.
- **NZS 4229 for a retaining wall.** Retaining walls are outside that standard's scope. A BOINZ member catches it instantly.

## 2. Leaks versus seismic: the verdict

**Leaks wins, and it is not close. But not as the licensing gap. As the narrative frame and the proof that the product works.**

**Why leaks wins on story:** PwC's 2009 report for the Department of Building and Housing estimated 42,000 failures and NZ$11.3 billion in 2008 dollars, with owners carrying 69% ($7.8 billion) and councils 25%. MBIE's own dispute-resolution material repeats both figures, so they are government-endorsed rather than consultant output. Everyone in a New Zealand building room understands it without explanation, and the council share means a council audience feels it personally.

**Why leaks cannot carry the licensing ask:** weathertightness is E2/AS1 territory. E2/AS1 is Crown-owned and already in Soterra's corpus. The standards dependencies inside E2 are real but narrow and mostly deflectable: wind zone and exposure zone route to NZS 3604 but BRANZ Maps supplies an approximate answer free; stucco routes wholesale to NZS 4251.1, which is the strongest single handoff in the document; timber treatment routes to NZS 3602 but B2/AS1 modifies it. So leaks is where Soterra looks **good**, which is exactly why it should open the video, not close it.

**Why seismic loses for this specific pitch, on four counts:**

1. **The marquee seismic standards are already free.** NZS 4219:2009, NZS 1170.5:2004 (excl. A1), NZS 3604:2011, NZS 4229:2013, NZS 3101.1&2:2006, NZS 3404 Parts 1 and 2:1997 are all sponsored. A "we cannot answer, it is locked away" seismic demo gets corrected on the spot.
2. **The genuinely paid seismic cluster is mostly joint AS/NZS, which Standards NZ cannot license alone.** AS/NZS 2785:2020 (suspended ceilings, NZ$138.71 ex GST) and AS/NZS 1170.0:2002 are joint standards. Standards NZ does not own the copyright. The only pure-NZ paid seismic item is NZS 4541:2020 (automatic fire sprinkler systems, NZ$131.63 ex GST confirmed), which is a narrow sprinkler-bracing question.
3. **The failure data is commercial fitout, not Adam's market.** The MBIE/EQC survey (89% of ceilings, 85% of partitions and fire protection, $63,681 per floor) is 41 floors in 20 commercial buildings. Soterra's buyers are small residential and light commercial NZ firms. Restraint of ducting is not their daily question.
4. **Seismic salience is being legislated down in the biggest market.** The EPB amendment would remove around 2,900 buildings, 55% of EPBs, including all of Auckland, Northland and the Chatham Islands, with Auckland's removal taking effect immediately on Royal Assent. Building a council pitch around EPB right now means building on ground that is actively moving.

**The honest third answer:** neither leaks nor seismic is the licensing story. **The licensing story is residential structural and life-safety lookups: NZS 3604 Section 5, Section 6 and Section 8, NZS 4223.3, NZS 4514.** That is where the Crown content stops dead with nothing to fall back on. Leaks is the frame. NZS 3604 is the gap.

**One thing to fix in your own evidence before you use it anywhere.** PRODUCT-STUDY.md is being described as "27 council reports". It is 27 Auckland Council inspection outcome statements for **one consent**, a Hobsonville multi-unit project, 12 Dec 2023 to 3 Sep 2024. 14 Partial Pass, 6 Pass, 4 Fail, 3 Completed. The 22% clean-pass and 18-of-27 return-visit arithmetic is correct, but n = one project. Say "27 inspections across a single Auckland consent" and it survives scrutiny. Worse, the actual failed items were flashings at junctions, cavity battens as per plan, saddle flashings, deck threshold step-down, 150 mm membrane upstands, oversize holes in GIB braces and GBTLA screws at 300 centres. Every one of those is weathertightness, plan-lookup or manufacturer specification, all of which Soterra already holds. **Not one is a bracing unit, lintel size, pile spacing, safety glass threshold or smoke alarm position.** Do not attach the 22% figure to the standards demo questions. It does not support them, and a council reader will notice.

## 3. Who Adam is actually negotiating with

### CLNZ, now Tāwhia, cannot license this. Stop waiting on them.

Copyright Licensing New Zealand rebranded to **Tāwhia Copyright Aotearoa on 15 July 2026**. copyright.co.nz now returns HTTP 403; tawhia.nz is live. Address anything to Tāwhia.

Their Business Copyright Licence excludes Soterra's use case three separate ways, each verified verbatim on their site:

- **"Materials already in digital form (downloads, databases)"** is excluded. Sponsored standards are downloads.
- **"Digital born content from the internet"** is excluded.
- **"Works specifically excluded by the copyright owner"** is excluded, and Standards NZ has published exactly such an exclusion for AI tools.
- Plus the cap: *"up to 10% or one chapter of any work"*, and *"Licences currently cover print and physical formats."*

A 10%-of-one-work photocopying licence is not a right to index, retrieve from and reproduce extracts of a standard inside a commercial AI product. It is structurally the wrong instrument.

One honest caveat: I could not find a Tāwhia statement confirming Standards NZ is or is not a mandating publisher of theirs. That conclusion rests on absence of evidence. **The three structural blocks above are each independently verified and are sufficient on their own.**

Tāwhia's AI page is advocacy only, no AI licence product: *"the responsible development and use of AI, but not at the expense of creators' rights"*. They do say *"In 2026, Tāwhia will be rolling out the inclusion of a range of digital materials to the licensing schemes"*, so keep the relationship warm, but do not let it gate the build. **If they came back and are "looking into it", the useful reply is to thank them, ask specifically whether Standards New Zealand is a mandating publisher in their repertoire, and say you are approaching Standards NZ directly in parallel.** That is not going behind their back, it is the only route that exists.

### The real counterparty: Standards New Zealand, Digital Products Licensing

This is a business unit of MBIE holding Crown copyright, so **every NZS-prefixed standard is one counterparty**. Their published position, verbatim:

> "Standards content cannot be used in digital or AI-enabled tools without a licence. This includes: software, mobile apps and digital platforms; AI tools and LLMs (including prompts, outputs, or knowledge bases); internal compliance, training, or decision-support systems."

> "Uploading standards or standards content to public or third-party AI tools (such as ChatGPT) without permission is strictly prohibited."

And in the same breath, the invitation:

> "We support innovators looking to repurpose standards, or parts of them, in digital tools and apps that increase their usefulness."

Plus a second pathway that maps directly onto the council pitch: licensing pathways *"for business owners who want to build tools for their staff to use in applying standards, understanding standards or evaluating levels of standards compliance."*

Page last updated 25 March 2026. This is current, it is published, and it names Soterra's exact use case. **There is no published "no", only an unpublished process.**

**Programme terms, from a 2022 Standards NZ interview with the DPL programme lead:**
- No fee during the development phase. Verbatim: *"The fact that the development phase as it stands involves no fee, makes it easy for new entrants"*.
- Revenue share at commercialisation, under "a clear pricing structure".
- A condition that *"the standard itself is the authority"*, and users of a digital product based on a standard were required to purchase the standard itself.

That article is four years old and I could **not** verify the named programme lead is still in the role. Do not address a letter to him by name. Use **valueadd@standards.govt.nz** for the application and let them route it; **copyright@standards.govt.nz** is the copyright contact.

**The single most commercially important sentence I found anywhere**, from the Standards NZ NZS 3910:2023 reference guide page:

> "Customers of digital products licensees, however, do not need to purchase a licence from Standards New Zealand to use these products."

That removes the biggest objection to Soterra's business model: your paying builders would not each need their own Standards NZ licence. Note it sits in mild tension with the 2022 "users must purchase the standard itself" condition, so **ask them to confirm which applies**. It is a good-faith opener either way.

**Do not use the NZS 3910 adaptation licence prices as an anchor.** Yes, $6,705 unlimited-use and $1,856.25 fifteen-use are real. But the same page says those licences *"are not sufficient to create commercial products"*. Citing a licence they have explicitly ruled out for commercial products, as evidence of what they charge for commercial products, invites a one-line rebuttal and looks like you did not read the sentence under the price.

### Precedents to put in the letter

1. **SNZ HB 3604:2011, "Timber-framed buildings - Selected extracts from NZS 3604:2011"**. Standards NZ's own product: commonly-used-on-site figures and tables extracted from NZS 3604, sold with the disclaimer that it must not substitute for the full Standard. **This is the strongest single item you have. Lead with it.** You are asking to build the digital version of a product they already publish, with the same disclaimer attached.
2. **CCANZ CP 01:2014**, verbatim: *"Chapter 4.6 (Masonry Veneer), of this Code of Practice is based on content of NZS 4229:2013 ... and has been provided by Standards New Zealand under licence 001006."* Stronger than it looks: CP 01:2014 is the edition cited as **Acceptable Solution E2/AS3**, so the licensed reproduction is itself compliance-document content.
3. **Licence LN001327**, Standards NZ on behalf of ISO/IEC, published by SAM for Compliance for ISO/IEC 27002:2022. Two public licence numbers, not one.
4. **Licence LN001498** is the MBIE sponsorship licence number printed on sponsored standard pages. Quoting it shows you read the fine print.

### What joint AS/NZS ownership blocks, permanently

Verified verbatim from two directions:

> "Sponsored standards do not include any standards for which copyrighted content prevents Standards New Zealand entering a sponsorship agreement, including joint Australian/New Zealand standards (AS/NZS)."

> "International and joint Standards (such as AS/NZS) are not included in the sponsorship agreement as copyright for these documents isn't owned by Standards New Zealand."

Standards Australia's own FAQ requires a licence for *"any extract"*, defined as *"a sentence, paragraph, a diagram or figure, or a clause"*, offers 3, 6 and 12 month contract licensing terms, and is completely silent on AI, LLMs, machine learning or knowledge bases. No pathway, no policy, no stated position.

**Therefore: scope the first deal to NZS-prefixed standards only.** Say so explicitly in the letter. It shows you understand their rights position, it makes the ask smaller, and it avoids a second negotiation across the Tasman with a body that has published no AI pathway at all. It also means electrical is permanently closed for now, since every electrical item runs to AS/NZS 3000:2018 ($256.16 ex GST), which is consistent with what RESEARCH-CORPUS.md already concluded.

*(A separate claim floating around that the Australian Government has ruled out a text-and-data-mining exemption and is exploring paid licensing with legislation targeted for early 2027 is **unverified**. Do not repeat it.)*

### Does free-to-view offer a demo route that avoids paywalled content entirely?

**Yes, and it is the route to take.** Every one of the five recommended demo questions runs to a standard on MBIE's sponsored list: NZS 3604:2011, NZS 4223.3:2016, NZS 4514. None requires touching a purchase-only standard, and none requires touching a joint AS/NZS. That means:

- You never have to justify why you have a copy. It is free, government-funded, and you downloaded it the way MBIE intends.
- The only thing you are asking for is the reproduction right, on content the Crown already pays to give away.
- Standards NZ forgoes **no sales revenue** by granting it, because there is no PDF sale to lose on a sponsored title. There is no purchasable PDF for a sponsored standard at all; only hard copies are sold. (Careful: NZS 3604:2011 hard copy is $532.02 and NZS 4229:2013 is $304.88. Those are **hard copy** prices. Calling them PDF prices in front of Standards NZ misdescribes their own product line.)

One live oddity worth raising as a question rather than an assertion: **NZS 2295:2006** appears on MBIE's sponsored list but its shop page sells a PDF at $193.39 with no sponsorship badge, unlike the four sponsored pages I checked where the PDF variant is simply absent from sale. Either sponsorship lapsed after the December 2024 list or the page is not reflecting it. Ask, do not assert.

## 4. The legal shape of the demo

### The rule you cannot break

Standards NZ has published, in plain words, that AI tools and LLMs including **prompts, outputs, or knowledge bases** require a licence, and that uploading standards content to third-party AI tools is strictly prohibited. Copyright still applies in full to sponsored standards. The Copyright Act 1994 exposure they cite is a fine of up to $150,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years.

**Practical consequence, and this one is aimed at how you actually work:** do not paste a standard PDF into Claude, ChatGPT or any hosted model to "just check what the table says" while prototyping. That is the specific act their page names. It is also the act most likely to happen accidentally in the next two weeks.

**Do not ingest any standard text into the Soterra corpus before a licence exists.** Not in an index, not in an embedding store, not in a scratch folder on the deploy. The knowledge-base clause covers it.

### The four options, assessed

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Free-to-view / sponsored standards only** | Necessary but not sufficient. Sponsorship removes the access barrier, not the reproduction barrier. Use it to pick the questions, not to justify reproduction. |
| **Building Code references without reproducing standard text** | **Fully lawful and this is the "before" half of your demo.** F2/AS1 clause 1.1.1, C/AS1 clause 2.2.1.2, E2/AS1 clause 9.1.2.7, B1/VM1 clause 2.1.1.1 are Crown content Soterra can quote in full. CodeHub's clause numbering and citation graph are published under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 by MBIE and give you section titles and structure with no clause text. One honest ambiguity: whether a standard's table of contents on CodeHub is MBIE Crown material under that CC BY grant or excluded third-party material is genuinely unclear. **Ask Standards NZ to confirm in writing. It is a cheap, credible opener.** |
| **Clearly labelled mock or illustrative answer** | **Yes, with the content withheld rather than invented.** Render the full citation card shell (standard number, edition, section, clause, page reference, "the standard is the authority" footer, link to the free PDF) with the extract area redacted and watermarked "ILLUSTRATIVE, CONTENT WITHHELD PENDING LICENCE". Never fabricate a plausible-looking figure into a mockup you show a rights holder. That is worse than showing nothing. |
| **Private account-gated demo shown only to the rights holder** | **Mandatory, whatever else you do.** Clerk-gated, unlisted, no public URL, no YouTube link, one account issued to Standards NZ and one to Tāwhia if they want it. Soterra already runs behind Clerk so this is a config decision, not a build. |

### The recommendation

**Build a three-layer, account-gated demo, ask for a development licence, and let them unlock the real thing.**

1. **Layer one, real and complete.** The E2/AS1 ground clearance answer, working end to end with cited extract and page image. Crown content only. Proves the product.
2. **Layer two, real and incomplete.** The lintel question answered honestly as Soterra answers it today. Nothing reproduced. This is not a mockup, it is the live product, and the fact that it stops is the entire argument.
3. **Layer three, labelled mockup.** The same lintel answer rendered as a licensed answer would render it, with the citation shell complete and the extract withheld and watermarked. Twenty seconds of video.

Then the ask, which is the part most people get wrong: **do not ask for permission to demo. Apply for a development licence.** Their published programme charges no fee during development. So the letter says: here is the product, here are the five questions it cannot complete, here is the SNZ HB 3604 precedent, here is licence 001006, we propose to scope this to NZS-prefixed standards only, and we are applying for a no-fee development licence with revenue share agreed at commercialisation on your published structure.

**Build their conditions in before they ask.** Every standards-touching answer carries: "This is an extract. The standard itself is the authority", a direct link to the free sponsored PDF or the shop page, the edition stamp, and the clause or table number. And show them your **version control**: corpus is edition-pinned, DZ NZS 3604 is out for consultation with submissions closing **8 September 2026**, and Soterra has a re-licensing and re-indexing path for the new edition. That directly answers a rights holder's central fear about AI ingestion, which is that content gets frozen, drifts, and misleads someone.

Two extra beats that cost nothing and buy credibility:
- Note that E2/AS1's own Appendix A prints NZS 2295's title as "Pliable, Permeable Building Membranes" while the correct title is "Pliable, permeable building underlays". You read both.
- Note that the E2/AS1 3rd edition transition window closed on **31 July 2026**, three days ago, so every 3rd-edition clause number still circulating in council checklists and BRANZ articles is now stale for new consents. That is the single freshest argument in the pack for a maintained, edition-aware citation tool. **Verify Soterra's corpus holds the 4th edition, and B1/VM1 Second Edition, before you record.**

Finally, if Standards NZ says no or goes quiet, **the fallback is still a real product**: routing. Soterra tells a builder which standard, which section, and gives the free download link. That is genuinely more than most of them do today, and it is lawful with no licence at all.

## 5. The council opportunity

### Realistic sizing: this is 69 organisations, not a volume market

As at **March 2026** there are **69 Building Consent Authorities**: 66 territorial or regional authorities, two private BCAs (BCAL Limited and Farsight), and one independent division of Kāinga Ora (Consentium). Date-stamp that figure every time, because it moves: 67 in September 2024, 68 in July 2025, 69 in March 2026.

Volume for scale: **Q1 2026 saw 16,532 building consent applications and 9,902 CCC applications processed nationally**, overall median processing nine working days, 95.5% within statutory period.

**There is no reliable current national count of building inspectors. Do not quote one.** The 2018 Census figure routes through a decommissioned government website. BOINZ's "over 1200 members engaged in Building Surveying and Building Controls in both the public and private sectors" is the usable proxy, and MBIE's own words are a better citation for the shortage anyway: *"In 2021, a national accreditation report found that BCAs were finding it increasingly necessary to work together to deal with staff shortages and skills gaps."*

**Honest sizing statement:** 69 buyers cannot be a volume business. Councils are a **credibility and price-per-account market**, not a headcount market. Whatever the per-BCA price ends up being, it is unknowable until the first deal, and any number in a deck before then is invented. What you can say is that the sale is enterprise-shaped: small logo count, long cycles, high reference value, and a price point well above the NZ$149/299/599 SME bands.

### The evidence to lead a council conversation with

MBIE's own evaluation of the building consent system, all first-party survey data:

- **96% of BCAs say applicants expect BCAs to perform their quality checking.** This is the sharpest single statement of the problem Soterra solves.
- **82% say sector professionals do not understand the Building Code** and consider it a big issue.
- Over 50% say site supervisors are unavailable during inspections.
- ~75% of sector respondents say BCA conduct is overly risk-averse.
- 36% of both sides call inconsistent IT systems a big issue; BCAs average **2.4 platforms** with over a dozen distinct platforms nationally.
- 55% of sector respondents cite excessive documentation; **stand-alone house consents run 700 to 1,600 pages**. That number alone justifies retrieval over search.

And MBIE's own regulatory impact statement, verbatim: *"Inconsistencies and unpredictability within BCAs, where different Building Consent Officers request varying information for similar projects, create confusion, delay projects, and increase costs."* The same RIS lists "automated checking tools" among the innovations the reforms are meant to encourage.

On defect cost, **use $995 million a year (range $420m to $1.74bn), NZIER for BRANZ**. Do **not** use $2.5 billion. That figure is the modelled GDP uplift if all defects were eliminated, which the report itself calls unrealistic and labels "the size of the prize". Several news outlets got this wrong and a council economist will check it. Note the study is modelled on 2018 data using 2014 prevalence.

On pre-inspection checks: a field trial across **193 framing inspections** saw first-time failure fall from about 32% to about 27% when builders used standards-based quality checks before the council inspection. Say **"a small field trial suggests"**. 193 inspections and a 5 percentage point shift is unlikely to reach statistical significance, and the underlying PhD appears unpublished. The author is at AUT, which is a warm-introduction angle you are already inside.

### Who to approach, in order

1. **BOINZ.** Over 1,200 members across public and private sectors, nine branches, training and events. This is the single most valuable relationship: distribution, credibility, and a route into councils that is not a procurement process. Start here.
2. **Waikato Building Consent Group via Co-Lab.** Eight councils (Hamilton City, Hauraki, Matamata-Piako, Otorohanga, Thames-Coromandel, Waikato, Waipa, Waitomo), operating since 2004 with a group Quality Assurance System, shared documentation and **shared legislative interpretation**. One conversation reaches eight BCAs, and shared interpretation is exactly what a cited-answer tool serves. *(Drop the "17 councils joint procurement" figure, it is not supported by the source.)*
3. **Private BCAs: BCAL and Farsight.** Two logos, no procurement rules, fast. Their endorsement transfers to councils because they carry the same accreditation obligations. Farsight is tied to Summerset, so treat it as one client relationship.
4. **Consentium (Kāinga Ora).** A single Crown decision-maker already comfortable buying software, and it posted a one-day median for both BCs and CCCs at 100% within timeframe in Q1 2026. **Important correction to how this has been framed:** Consentium consents buildings only where *"Kāinga Ora will own and retain the building"*. It is an in-house BCA for its parent's portfolio, not proof that a national open-market non-council BCA works. Do not oversell it in a pitch.
5. **Canterbury.** Christchurch City, Waimakariri and Selwyn signed an MoU reported in May 2026, covering consistent processes, mutual support on paperwork and inspections, and joint work on large projects. Contacts named in reporting: John Higgins (Christchurch), Robert Love (Selwyn), Kelly LaValley (Waimakariri). **Accuracy warning:** the MoU commits to sharing *"training and development and subscriptions"*, not "system subscriptions". Do not present it as an intent to buy software jointly. A Christchurch procurement officer has the actual text.
6. **Mackenzie District Council**, opportunistically. **117 of 151 inspections failed in June 2026, 77.5%.** Use 77.5%, and attribute the "80% failure is not good enough" line to Councillor Philippa Guerin as her rounding, not as council data. Their building control officer attributed failures to complex framing designs around Tekapo, incomplete inspections and missing documentation, spread across multiple builders and designers. A council publicly embarrassed by framing failures is a warm door, and framing is exactly where NZS 3604 lives. *(The claim that their operations manager called the rate "typical nationally" is unverified. Do not repeat it.)*

### Procurement path

- **Councils are only encouraged, not mandated, to apply the Government Procurement Rules.** Regional councils and reserve boards are "expected to apply"; the territorial city and district authorities are "encouraged to apply". Phrase this correctly to a council audience or it reads as though you have misunderstood their status. Practically it means dozens of individual procurement policies, most of which mirror the Rules.
- **Rule 10 open-advertise threshold is $100,000** for goods and services, assessed on **maximum total estimated value**. A three-year deal at $40k a year is a $120k procurement, not a $40k one. Structure term and renewals deliberately, or accept a GETS listing. *(That the 5th edition took effect 1 December 2025 is unverified; the thresholds themselves are current.)*
- **The shortcut: councils are eligible to buy off All-of-Government and Collaborative Contracts.** That asymmetry, many doors in and one bypass, is the single most useful procurement fact here.
- **Pae Hokohoko / Marketplace**: 1,065 registered suppliers, 2,715 published listings, 285 registered agencies, applications open any time and done entirely online. **But Marketplace's own site does not name councils or local government as eligible buyers.** Council access rests on the procurement.govt.nz AoG listing, not on anything Marketplace says. **Call DIA and confirm before treating Marketplace as the route to councils.**

### Are private inspectors an easier beachhead?

**Easier sale, wrong beachhead.**

NZIBS holds "over 200 registered, transitional and student members", and that count **includes students**, so the practising population is under 200. The NZIBI figure of 83 is unverified. The private surveyor market is too small to fund anything and its testimonials do not automatically transfer to a BCA audience.

**The real beachhead is BOINZ plus one shared-service cluster.** BOINZ gives credibility and reach across both public and private inspectors in one relationship. Waikato/Co-Lab gives a paid pilot across eight BCAs from a single decision. Use private surveyors and the two private BCAs for speed, validation and quotes, not as the revenue base.

### The 2025-2026 reform angle, honestly assessed

**Real and confirmed:**
- **Building Amendment Bill, first reading 2 July 2026, select committee submissions close 15 November 2026.** Contents include the shift from joint and several to proportionate liability, mandatory home warranties for new builds and major renovations over $100,000, professional indemnity insurance for design professionals, a 10-day fast-track consenting pathway, PIM processing cut from 20 to 10 working days, expanded granny flat and offsite construction options, and enabling councils to consolidate or collaborate on BCA functions. *(The 29 June introduction date, the vote breakdown, the one-year implementation period and 2028 commencement are all unverified. Cite the 2 July first reading only.)*
- MBIE also flags **combining the Building Research Levy with the Building Levy under a more contestable funding model**. That matters to you: the Building Research Levy funds the BRANZ and ModelDocs research the whole pitch leans on.
- **Regulation 7A, in force 22 August 2025.** BCA policies and procedures must ensure at least 80% of inspections are carried out within three working days of the Date for Inspection Requested. MBIE explicitly names risk-based prioritisation and **increased use of remote inspections** as acceptable ways to meet it.

**The reform angle that does NOT work, and you must not walk into a council with it:**

- **"We help you hit the three-day rule" is dead.** First results (Q3 2025 data, published December 2025): **66 of 67 BCAs met the target**, 97.3% of 67,000 inspections completed within three working days, roughly 57,900 same day. They already pass. Sell inspection **quality and consistency** instead.
- **"Proportionate liability makes us your liability defence" is backwards for councils.** The RIS is explicit that BCAs currently act as deep-pocket defendants and that under proportionate liability *"BCAs will only be responsible for their contribution to building defects and will no longer play the deep-pocket role"*. The reform is designed to **reduce** the exposure you would be selling protection against. The liability-defence pitch gets stronger for **builders and designers** and weaker for councils. The surviving council argument is that apportionment fights are won on evidence of what each party checked against which clause, so a timestamped cited-extract trail becomes more valuable. **That is my inference, not something the RIS says. Label it as such if you use it.**
- The claim that **Regulation 8** requires BCAs to actively fill capability gaps including enough inspectors for reg 7A is **unverified**. building.govt.nz will not serve to automated fetching. Since that claim does real work (it converts a discretionary software purchase into accreditation evidence), **someone should open the page in a browser and copy the exact sentence before it goes in any sales document.** If it holds up, it is the strongest procurement lever available.
- IANZ has **three** accreditation assessors covering **77 active accreditations**, with 214 technical expert days allocated for FY 2025/26 and zero new accreditations. Thin regulator capacity is a real backdrop, but do not overstate it.

**Competitors, cited to their own sites:** ConsentIQ (consentiq.nz), Building Consent Check (buildingconsentcheck.co.nz), ConsentNZ (consentnz.com), AI Build (aibuild.co.nz) and PermitBot (permitbot.co.nz) are all live. TeamBench unconfirmed. **None was verified as holding a Standards NZ licence.** All are on the pre-lodgement consent-checking side. A licensed standards corpus is the defensible difference, and it is the one thing none of them can copy quickly.

## Numbers and claims to strike from every document

Each of these is either wrong, misattributed, or unsupported. Any one of them, spotted by the audience it is aimed at, costs more than it is worth.

- **"NZS 3604 is paywalled."** It is free under MBIE sponsorship.
- **"$2.5 billion a year in building defects."** That is the modelled GDP uplift from eliminating all defects. The cost estimate is $995 million (range $420m to $1.74bn).
- **"$47 billion leaky homes."** An author's extrapolation applying PwC's 2009 cost ratio to an unreleased 2014 report. Unusable.
- **"Five in six affected dwellings never entered the WHRS system."** Mixes 7,377 claims against 42,000 dwellings. The like-for-like comparison is 12,810 properties, so roughly seven in ten. Fewer than a third were covered.
- **"20,000+ inspections fail each year nationally."** Internally inconsistent with the same source's own Auckland figures.
- **"95% seismic testing failure rate."** Stated with no attribution at all on a seismic-restraint vendor page.
- **"A third of Auckland new builds fail final inspection."** No primary source found.
- **"Auckland's failure rate was up to 40%."** That was a single week's peak in 2015; the monthly figure was 29%. And it is eleven years old.
- **"MBIE found 66% of RFIs are missing documents"** presented alongside the ModelDocs 66% as two data points. They are the same number. MBIE adopted the University of Auckland finding. The value is the official adoption, not a second measurement.
- **"Determination 2025/027 went the council's way."** MBIE agreed there were grounds to refuse and then **reversed** Auckland Council because the refusal letter failed section 50. Useful as evidence of NZS 3604 dependency, since the council's own case rested on it. Damaging if you get the outcome backwards.
- **"BRANZ found 40% of houses failing NZS 3604 on frame straightness."** BRANZ explicitly classed it as a quality defect, not a compliance one, and says most were only just over the tolerance.
- **"BRANZ SR335 found head flashing problems in 40%."** BRANZ says only "a BRANZ research project a few years ago", with no study number. Attribute to BRANZ, never to SR335.
- **"NZS 3604 covers two storeys and 10 m."** MBIE says up to 10 m and a maximum of three storeys, the third as a part storey within the roof space, with conditions.
- **"AS/NZS 2785 does not address earthquake actions."** True of the superseded 2000 edition. The 2020 edition added seismic content.
- **"NZS 1170.5 is paid."** The un-amended Building-Code-cited version is sponsored and free. Amendment 1 is not.
- **"$304.88 / $232.88 PDF prices."** Those are hard copy prices. No PDF is sold for a sponsored standard.
- **The NZS 3910 licence prices as a commercial anchor.** Standards NZ says those licences are not sufficient to create commercial products.
- **"Nick Ascroft, Digital Products Licensing programme lead."** Four-year-old source, tenure unverified. Write to valueadd@standards.govt.nz.
- **The $160 million Oaks Shores claim, Simpli's council membership count, Auckland's "400 remote inspections a month", the NZIBI membership figure, and the MEPS restraint thresholds (50 mm pipes, 150 mm, 400 mm trays, 10 kg ceiling items).** All unverified. The 10 kg figure in particular appears to come from NZS 1170.5 Table 8.1, not NZS 4219.
